**Part A: Collection and instrumentation (\~7/15)**

**Strengths**

* Genuine recordings from 16 speakers on 12 device labels, in four noise conditions, across WhatsApp (.opus, .ogg), .m4a, .aac, .mpeg and .mp4 sources.

* An FFmpeg plus energy-based VAD pipeline normalises everything to 16 kHz mono WAV, producing 284 clips. Speaker, region, device, noise condition and language-mix metadata exist for every clip, and a consent register has 16 rows.

**Gaps**

* Total speech is 1.25 hours, below the brief's 2-hour minimum. The report discloses this.

* Gujarati is almost absent. 85.9% of clips are pure English, with 22 English-Hindi, 13 pure Hindi, 5 English-Gujarati and no pure Gujarati clips, against a brief that asks for English, Hindi and Gujarati mixes.

* Speaker coverage is very uneven, from 2 clips (SPK06) to 44 clips (SPK09). Three of the 16 speakers are the report's authors.

* Metadata lacks a tool-version column; collector\_id is the constant "real\_recording" and the timestamp is the processing time, not the recording time.

* Anonymisation fails. metadata\_real.csv has a speaker\_name column with first names, the consent register shows first name plus initial as "anonymised\_name", and the metadata stores absolute local paths such as /home/rajdeepvraj/Documents/....

* There is no data-flow diagram and no collection log.

**Part B: Validation (\~9/20)**

**Strengths**

The check names also differ (duration\_mismatch versus plausible\_duration).

* The corrupted-data tests import their check functions from src/validation/validate\_dataset.py rather than from postprocess\_real.py, where the report says the real suite lives.

**Part C: Labelling and agreement (\~5/25)**

This is the most serious problem in the submission.

**What the report claims:** two annotators labelled the real campus recordings in Label Studio, with inter-annotator WER falling from 8.77% to 0.53% and intent kappa rising from 0.686 to 0.955 after the guideline revision, plus ten disagreement case studies.

**What the files show**

* The "Label Studio exports" were generated by code. simulate\_annotator\_records() in src/labelling/run\_agreement\_analysis.py builds both annotators' outputs from a reference transcript and intent, using hard-coded lists of clip indices where disagreements are injected (for example idx in \[1, 5, 8, 12, 16, ...\] in round 1 and \[14, 42\] in round 2). Its own comments mention target kappa values, and the exports contain a ground\_truth\_intent field that real Label Studio exports do not have.

* The labelled clips (AUD\_SPK01\_0001 and so on) do not exist in the real dataset. The real clips are REAL\_SPK01\_0001 and so on, and no AUD\_ clip appears in metadata\_real.csv. ENVIRONMENT.md lists gTTS (Google text-to-speech) and a corpus-generation prompt file is present, which points to an earlier synthetic corpus.

* The agreement files are dated 17 October, two days before the real recordings were processed (19 October).

* The two rounds cover different clips (AUD\_SPK01\_0001 to 0060 and AUD\_SPK02\_0061 onwards), so the kappa "improvement" cannot be attributed to the guideline revision even in principle.

* Only 60 clips per round were labelled, and none of the real clips were double-annotated, against the brief's 100\.

* The report's ten case studies (clips 0052, 0078, 0064, VPN, Turnitin, kettle, and so on) do not appear in the backing file 10\_detailed\_disagreements.md, which describes different AUD\_ clips.

* On the real data, transcripts are faster-whisper "tiny" output, intent domains come from a keyword classifier that falls back to random.choice when no keyword matches, and language labels come from keyword markers. HANDOFF\_A2.md says the transcripts were "reviewed by 2 annotators", but no evidence of that review is in the submission.

* The gold test set has 14 clips (the SPK11 and SPK12 in-distribution test split). The report claims "manual adjudication"; no adjudication record exists. At least one gold clip about internet connectivity is labelled hostel\_mess.

* The raw exports include personal institute email addresses.

**Credit:** guidelines v1 and v2, the Label Studio config and a labelling protocol exist.

I am describing what the code and files show; the professor should ask the team how these exports were produced.

**Part D: Augmentation and active learning (\~8/20)**

**Strengths**

* Four augmentations (additive noise, reverberation, 1.1x speed, telephony codec) and a Whisper-tiny benchmark: clean WER 47.3%, rising to 79.8% to 110.1% under augmentation.

* Active learning versus random across 3 budgets (20, 40, 80\) with 5 seeds, computed in code (the table matches the CSV).

**Gaps**

* The Whisper benchmark uses only 10 clips (the clean-condition standard deviation is 1.0), and the report does not state this.

* "MUSAN" noise is filtered Gaussian noise and the "RIR" is a synthetic exponentially decaying noise burst. No MUSAN corpus or real room impulse responses are used, which the brief names.

* The brief asks for selection by low decoder confidence. The active-learning code instead takes the first n clips per class using the true labels, so it is an oracle class-balanced sample, not an informativeness score. The labels it relies on come from the keyword classifier.

* Macro-F1 is low and non-monotonic (0.246, 0.296, 0.273), and the numbers are hard-coded into the report scripts.

* The brief asks for labels to survive augmentation checks; none are reported.
