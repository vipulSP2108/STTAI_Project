%% ── ABSTRACT ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Deploying speech-to-intent voice assistance in multilingual Indian campus environments
faces acute domain adaptation challenges from heavy code-mixing (English, Hindi, Gujarati),
device acoustic variability, and ambient hostel/cafeteria reverberation. In this work,
we present an end-to-end engineered audio collection, validation, and provenance pipeline
for Project~5 of CS203. We collect 1.255 hours of real student queries across 19 participants
(249 canonical 16\,kHz mono WAV clips, mean duration 18.14\,s). We implement an automated
14-check validation suite with \texttt{pytest} and \texttt{pandera}, achieving 100\% pass
on calibrated audio and catching 12/12 adversarial corruptions. A two-stage Label Studio
protocol improves inter-annotator consensus (Cohen's $\kappa$: $0.8446 \rightarrow 0.9889$;
WER: $0.002 \rightarrow 0.000$). We evaluate off-the-shelf Whisper across 5 synthetic distortion
families (1.058--1.679 WER) and simulate active learning uncertainty sampling, achieving
93.1\% accuracy at budget $B = 30$ clips vs.\ 86.3\% for random sampling. Finally, we establish
strict speaker-disjoint partitions (Train: 171, Val: 17, Test: 29, Cohort B: 32) with an
immutable 29-clip Gold Test Set frozen under SHA-256 for downstream modeling.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Voice-driven spoken language understanding (SLU) in Indian university settings presents
acute challenges at the intersection of speech recognition and data engineering. Students
spontaneously code-mix English, Hindi, and Gujarati, while recording queries across diverse
hardware modalities (budget smartphones, laptops, earphones) under varying acoustic
environments (quiet dorm rooms, reverberant dining halls, and outdoor corridors).

Commercial automated speech recognition (ASR) systems tuned exclusively on native monolingual
corpora degrade severely when exposed to code-switched technical vocabulary (e.g.,
\emph{``mess rebate portal''}, \emph{``SPI CPI transcript issue''}). To establish a rigorous
foundation for spoken intent routing, this study addresses five core data-centric challenges:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Provenance \& Calibration}: Standardizing heterogeneous multi-format student audio into calibrated 16\,kHz WAV.
  \item \textbf{Automated Verification}: Enforcing strict acoustic, SNR, and schema constraints via automated testing.
  \item \textbf{Consensus Annotation}: Multi-rater Label Studio protocol with guideline calibration.
  \item \textbf{Acoustic Robustness}: Benchmarking Whisper zero-shot WER under environmental perturbations.
  \item \textbf{Active Selection}: Demonstrating label efficiency via uncertainty-based query strategies.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Data Collection \& Preparation (Part A)}
\label{sec:data}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Campus Domain \& Demographics}

Acoustic data collection was executed across 19 campus participants (undergraduate,
postgraduate, and staff volunteers) at IIT Gandhinagar. Recordings capture realistic student
queries spanning six operational intent categories: \emph{academic\_affairs},
\emph{hostel\_facilities}, \emph{mess\_dining}, \emph{it\_support}, \emph{administrative},
and \emph{library}. Table~\ref{tab:demographics} summarizes dataset demographics.

\subsection{Ingestion, VAD and Normalization}

Raw audio was ingested from multiple formats (AAC, M4A, MP3, WAV). Audio streams were
converted to mono, resampled to 16\,kHz using band-limited sinc interpolation via
\texttt{torchaudio}, and processed with Voice Activity Detection (VAD) to trim non-informative
leading and trailing silences exceeding 300\,ms. Each waveform $x(t)$ was peak-normalized to $-1.0$\,dBFS:

\begin{equation}
  \tilde{x}(t) = x(t) \cdot 10^{\frac{-1.0 - \text{peak\_dBFS}}{20}}
  \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cryptographic Provenance \& Privacy}

To guarantee tamper-proof reproducibility, every audio clip is indexed with its SHA-256
hash in \texttt{data\_manifest.json}. All speaker identities are pseudonymized into identifiers
\texttt{P01}--\texttt{P19}, with personal phone numbers, student IDs, and EXIF tags permanently stripped.

\section{Benchmarking \& Active Learning (Part D)}
\label{sec:benchmarking}
%% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Acoustic Perturbation Benchmarks}

To benchmark acoustic robustness, we generated 60 verified label-preserving augmented variants
across 5 perturbation techniques: MUSAN cafeteria noise~\cite{snyder2015musan}, Room Impulse Response (RIR) reverberation,
tempo perturbation ($\pm 15\%$), pitch shifting ($\pm 2$ semitones), and MP3 compression (32\,kbps).
Table~\ref{tab:perturbations} reports off-the-shelf Whisper~\cite{radford2023robust} Base WER under acoustic distortions.

\begin{table}[h]
  \caption{Whisper Zero-Shot Acoustic Benchmark Under Distortions}
  \label{tab:perturbations}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Acoustic Condition} & \textbf{WER (Mean)} & \textbf{CER (Mean)} \\
    \midrule
    Clean Calibrated Baseline & 1.058 & 0.684 \\
    + Additive Cafeteria Noise (5\,dB SNR) & 1.428 (+35.0\%) & 0.892 \\
    + RIR Reverberation ($RT_{60} = 0.5$\,s) & 1.312 (+24.0\%) & 0.814 \\
    + Tempo Perturbation (0.85$\times$--1.15$\times$) & 1.185 (+12.0\%) & 0.742 \\
    + Pitch Perturbation ($\pm 2$ st) & 1.142 (+7.9\%) & 0.718 \\
    + MP3 Codec Compression (32\,kbps) & 1.214 (+14.7\%) & 0.765 \\
    \textbf{Composite Distortions Average} & \textbf{1.257 (+18.8\%)} & \textbf{0.789} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Active Learning Query Simulation}

We simulated an active learning acquisition loop~\cite{settles2010active} comparing Least-Confidence Uncertainty Sampling
against a Random Selection baseline over 5-fold cross-validation. Figure~\ref{fig:active_learning}
shows that uncertainty sampling reaches \textbf{93.1\% accuracy at $B = 30$ clips}, compared to
$B = 40$ clips for random selection (delivering a 25\% reduction in manual labeling effort).

\section{Dataset Card (Gebru et al. Datasheet)}
\label{app:datasheet}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Motivation}: Created to benchmark cross-lingual speech-to-intent classification under realistic Indian campus acoustics (code-mixing, device diversity, hostel noise).
  \item \textbf{Composition}: 249 segmented audio recordings (1.255 hours, mean 18.14\,s) paired with verified intent labels (6 classes: academic, hostel, mess, IT, admin, library) and human reference transcripts.
  \item \textbf{Collection Process}: Recorded between Oct 8 and Oct 18, 2026 across 19 campus volunteers using smartphone and laptop microphones in quiet, cafeteria, and hostel settings. Formal consent obtained; PII permanently stripped.
  \item \textbf{Preprocessing \& QA}: WebRTC VAD silence trimming ($<300$\,ms), peak normalization to $-1.0$\,dBFS, 14-check validation suite with \texttt{pandera}, 100\% pass on calibrated data, and 12/12 adversarial corruptions detected.
  \item \textbf{Uses \& Governance}: Designed for academic SLU research. Version-controlled via DVC and Git. Audio binaries indexed via SHA-256 in \texttt{data\_manifest.json}.
\end{enumerate}
